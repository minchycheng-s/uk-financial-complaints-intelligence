# Evidence-based business analysis

## Purpose and scope

This analysis answers practical business questions using the current processed outputs. The analytical unit is a **firm-product-reporting-period observation**, not an individual complaint or customer. The latest period is **2025-H2**.

The warning outputs are prioritisation aids. They do not prove misconduct, customer harm, or the cause of a change. Counts below therefore include denominators, and data-quality limitations are kept separate from risk signals.

## Executive answers

- The latest review workload contains **29 priority-review observations and 111 review observations**, together representing **140 of 578 observations (24.2%)**.
- A separate **74 observations (12.8%)** have insufficient data. They should enter a data-remediation queue, not be interpreted as low risk.
- Insurance and pure protection has the largest absolute priority workload: **13 priority cases** and **53 priority-or-review cases**. Decumulation and pensions has a similar priority rate, despite a much smaller population.
- **Eight of the 29 current priority observations (27.6%)** were also classified as priority in at least five reporting periods. These are sensible candidates for longitudinal review, but the periods are not necessarily consecutive.
- The latest priority cases are most commonly associated with high upheld percentages, high provision-context rates, and high over-eight-week closure percentages relative to peers. These overlapping rules explain selection; they do not explain why the underlying business performance occurred.
- The overall increase in opened complaints is dominated by Consumer Credit. Removing that product group changes the comparison from a **56.1% overall increase** to a **5.0% decrease** across the other five groups. This should be investigated as a possible population, reporting, or definition change before it is treated as a deterioration.
- Bank Rate and inflation provide context only. This project does not contain a causal design, so macroeconomic movements cannot be claimed to have caused complaint changes.

## 5. What complaint movement requires investigation?

| Product group | Opened complaints, 2025-H2 | Previous period | Change | Percentage change |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Consumer Credit | 3,805,146 | 1,756,172 | +2,048,974 | +116.7% |
| Banking and credit cards | 844,807 | 888,574 | -43,767 | -4.9% |
| Insurance and pure protection | 620,885 | 653,535 | -32,650 | -5.0% |
| Decumulation and pensions | 80,272 | 85,424 | -5,152 | -6.0% |
| Home finance | 66,543 | 69,356 | -2,813 | -4.1% |
| Investments | 39,931 | 43,333 | -3,402 | -7.9% |

Across all products, opened complaints increased by **1,961,190 (56.1%)**. Consumer Credit represents **69.7% of the latest total** and more than accounts for the net increase. Excluding Consumer Credit, the other five groups declined by **87,784 (5.0%)**.

The defensible business question is therefore not “why did all complaints rise?” It is “does the Consumer Credit series reflect a real comparable increase, or a change in reporting population, definitions, or source coverage?” The aggregate data alone cannot answer that question.

## 6. Can economic or external data explain the movement?

In 2025-H2, Bank Rate moved from **4.25% to 3.75%**, while the latest CPI and CPIH annual rates in the context table are **3.4% and 3.6%**. These series help describe the environment in which complaints were reported.

They do not establish causation. There are only ten half-year periods, no causal identification strategy, and several possible changes in firm population and reporting coverage. External context is therefore not included in the warning score.

The current Financial Ombudsman Service work is a taxonomy mapping, not an ingested case-outcome dataset. The project cannot yet use Ombudsman outcomes to corroborate firm-level FCA signals.

## Recommended next decisions

1. Allocate first-line review capacity to the 29 priority observations, with additional focus on insurance and pensions.
2. Review the eight recurrent priority observations longitudinally, checking source evidence and rule history.
3. Create a separate remediation queue for the 74 insufficient-data observations, starting with Home Finance.
4. Investigate Consumer Credit comparability before presenting its increase as business deterioration.
5. Treat macroeconomic measures as context and add causal language only if a suitable research design and additional controls are introduced.
6. Ingest appropriately licensed FOS outcome data before making cross-source customer-outcome claims.

## Reproducibility and limitations

The headline evidence register is saved in
[`data/processed/analysis/business_question_evidence.csv`](../data/processed/analysis/business_question_evidence.csv).
The calculations use:

- `data/processed/warnings/firm_product_warning_summary.csv`
- `data/processed/warnings/warning_indicators.csv`
- `data/processed/reporting/dashboard_product_period_context.csv`
- `data/processed/reporting/dashboard_economic_context.csv`

Important limitations remain: the data is aggregated, firm inclusion can vary, context measures have different denominators, source percentages above 100% are preserved where confirmed, unresolved firm identities remain, and no direct measure of complaint severity or customer exposure is available.
